# Papier-Mache Masks for Halloween 2026

An English production plan for **at least 200 saleable adult masks**, using three designs, a 3D-printed master for each design, reusable aluminium moulds, and a domestic oven.

**Plan date:** 12 October 2026. **Sales deadline:** before Halloween, Saturday 31 October 2026. **Production allowance:** 220 production starts, plus a small prototype allowance. Twenty extra starts are a rejection allowance, not a guarantee of 200 acceptable masks.

**Current status: planning and prototyping.** The shaped-tray mould, release surface, oven cycle, paper thickness, shrinkage, paint finish and elastic crimps have not been physically validated. Pass the prototype checks below before scaling up.

Terminology:

- **Mould 1 / master:** the 3D-printed positive face used for cold-forming. It does not go into the oven.
- **Mould 2 / production mould:** two shaped aluminium tray shells, mechanically joined and supported with aluminium wire.
- **Release liner:** a thin foil skin over Mould 2. It is separate from the structural aluminium trays.

## 1. Making the masks

### 1.1 Design and print Mould 1

1. Measure actual adult wearers and establish eye spacing, breathing openings, face clearance and strap position. Do not treat one generic face dimension as a universal adult fit.
2. Keep the first three designs shallow and simple. Avoid hooked noses, inward lips or other undercuts that trap a shell on a rigid positive mould.
3. Round edges and allow room for the paper thickness and measured drying shrinkage.
4. Print one supported master per design. Use enough wall strength for hand pressure during cold-forming; save material only where it does not make the master collapse.
5. Use the finished prototype to decide any scale correction. Do not apply an assumed universal paper shrinkage percentage.

Printing consumables are **excluded from the purchase budget at the owner's request**.

### 1.2 Cold-form and reinforce Mould 2

1. With the tray resting on the master, gradually press one aluminium tray into the face shape. Work separately on a second tray.
2. Nest the two shaped shells. Retain a generous outer flange; fold it over together around a perimeter wire. Add wire braces on the back where the shell flexes.
3. Use mechanical folds and captured wire ends to join the layers. No adhesive is specified between the trays.
4. Fold sharp edges away from the working face. Remove the plastic master before any heating.
5. Begin with one mould per design. After successful repeated drying/demoulding trials, make **nine moulds: three per design**, using 18 trays; keep two trays spare.

The selected tray listing gives inconsistent dimensions: its title says about 33 × 21.7 × 5.2 cm and a bullet gives about 31.5 × 21.4 × 4.3 cm. Inspect usable area before relying on a full-face fit.

Oven suitability of an intact food tray does **not** establish rigidity after reshaping. If the nose tears, the face dents or the mould progressively distorts, simplify the geometry or revise the reinforcement before making the remaining moulds.

### 1.3 Add and test the release liner

Layer order, outside to inside:

~~~text
Paper strips + flour paste             -> finished mask shell
Thin removable foil release liner      -> plain or tested non-stick foil
Two shaped aluminium tray shells       -> reusable Mould 2
Aluminium perimeter wire / rear braces -> structural support
~~~

- Press foil gently over the face and fold excess around the outer flange. Do not glue it down or wrap the paper itself around the back of the mould.
- **Plain foil is not a non-stick coating.** Its possible advantage is that it can lift with the shell and flex during peeling; this still needs a release test.
- For the **Bacofoil Non-Stick** trial, put the **non-shiny side towards the paper**. The manufacturer identifies this side as the food-safe silicone-coated surface. [Manufacturer instructions](https://www.bacofoil.co.uk/product/bacofoil-the-non-stick-kitchen-foil/)
- Compare release, wrinkles, liner damage and paint adhesion on prototype pieces. Reuse a liner only while it stays intact and continues releasing cleanly; do not assume an unlimited number of cycles.
- No industrial release wax or spray is included. For example, PARTALL Hi-Temp Wax is rated for resin processes up to about 177°C, but its solvent-containing formulation and application instructions do not establish suitability for this household paper-mask process. [Product information](https://rexco-usa.com/product/partall-hi-temp-wax/) and [application guide](https://rexco-usa.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/PARTALL-Hi-Temp-Wax.pdf)

If neither liner releases acceptably, stop and resolve that process problem before committing the remaining paper. An oven temperature rating alone does not prove compatibility with flour paste.

### 1.4 Make paste and lay up the paper

Use unprinted newsprint strips as the main structure. Tissue is not required; the local cotton patches provide reinforcement at stressed areas.

For a small cooked-paste trial, mix **2 tablespoons of plain white flour with 1 cup of cold water** until smooth. Heat while stirring, remove from the heat when it thickens and begins to bubble, then cool before use. Make fresh paste for each work session. This starting recipe comes from [Ultimate Paper Mache](https://www.ultimatepapermache.com/paper-mache-recipes); the production quantity and consistency must be set by the trial.

1. Tear manageable strips, using smaller pieces around the nose and eye area.
2. Brush on a thin film of paste. Smooth each strip down and remove pools of paste and trapped air.
3. Alternate strip directions and overlap joints. Trial roughly **4–6 thin layers** and record the actual paper mass; this is a starting comparison, not a validated final specification.
4. Embed cotton reinforcement between paper layers around each strap position. Allow about **30 × 50 mm per patch** and extend it beyond the intended slot. Use two patch layers locally if the pull test requires them.
5. Finish with a smooth paper layer and leave the intended outline accessible for later trimming.

Do not add loose fishing line or plastic netting to this oven process. Local cotton patches keep the structure simple and the material spend low. If the purchased lightweight cloth pulls apart, use denser clean cotton offcuts and repeat the anchor test.

### 1.5 Trial the drying process, then standardise it

**There is no verified drying time or validated oven recipe for this exact stack of materials.**

For a small attended trial only, use the oven's lowest controllable low-temperature mode, around **60–70°C if the appliance supports it**. This is a proposed starting setting, not a measured material temperature or a manufacturer-approved process. If the appliance cannot maintain a suitable low setting, use ventilated air drying rather than selecting a hotter setting to meet the schedule.

- Heat only the unpainted paper/paste/cotton shell on its aluminium support. Remove the printed master; add elastic, crimps and paint after drying.
- Keep foil and paper away from elements and flame, leave circulation gaps, and follow the appliance's normal ventilation instructions. Do not prop the door open unless its instructions allow it.
- Attend the process and check frequently, beginning with short intervals. Stop if there is scorching, unusual odour, bubbling or distortion.
- Aluminium blocks drying from the underside. When a shell can be lifted without losing shape, expose its inside to circulating air on a suitable support. Do not declare it dry merely because its outside feels dry.
- Let it cool before checking rigidity and residual dampness. If a kitchen scale is available, compare cooled weights after further drying; a stable reading is one useful check alongside a fully dry, firm interior.
- Repeat the successful cycle at six-mask loading, checking each shelf position. Record all additional drying passes in the capacity calculation.

No oven thermometer is included, as requested. The dial setting is not proof of the actual temperature, so a successful observed pilot is essential. Paint only when the shell is dry throughout; trapped moisture can cause later deterioration. [Drying guidance](https://www.ultimatepapermache.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-to-Make-Paper-Mache-The-Basic-Steps-compressed.pdf)

### 1.6 Trim, paint and fit the strap

1. Trim the outside edge and eye/breathing openings. Cover rough contact edges with a narrow pasted paper strip and dry that repair fully.
2. Use the acrylic paints in thin brushed coats after drying. Reserve a brush for paint so flour paste does not contaminate the finish.
3. Distribute designs across the 14 colours. The set contains only 280 ml of each colour: it is not enough to assume all masks can receive a heavy white or black base coat.
4. Let paint dry/cure according to its label. Check a sample for tackiness and colour transfer with a damp white cloth. Craft acrylic is not face paint; an acceptable dried finish must be checked on the actual mask.
5. Cut a rounded slot through the reinforced temple area, slightly wider than the **6 mm elastic**. Keep enough reinforced material between the slot and mask edge to prevent tearing; use the prototype pull test to establish its position.
6. Pass the elastic through the slot, fold it back approximately **20–25 mm**, and put the two stacked layers inside a **10 × 8 mm fold-over crimp**.
7. Fold the crimp wings inward using pliers. Grip the elastic, not the paper shell. Place the finished crimp outside the mask, with no sharp edge or metal resting against skin.
8. Trial fit before cutting all straps. The **60 cm per mask** figure is a purchasing allowance including folds and trimming, not the final strap length for every adult.

The crimp's advertised 8 mm width is not a certified internal clearance or elastic grip rating. Test the actual double thickness, repeated stretching, slipping and fibre damage before making all 440 attachments. If it cuts or slips, do not simply crush it harder; change the attachment method, such as sewing the folded overlap, and allow for thread and labour.

### 1.7 Final checks and storage

Check every finished mask for:

- Complete dryness, a stable shape and no mould or unusual odour.
- Clear eye and breathing openings, smooth edges and comfortable fit.
- Secure cotton-reinforced anchors, stable crimps and sound elastic.
- A dry finish without unacceptable tackiness or colour transfer.

Keep passed masks dry and ventilated until packaging. Count accepted stock by design. These are decorative adult masks for dry use; do not describe the materials as universally skin-safe, latex-free or nickel-free without evidence. Keep metal fittings off the face.

### Release-liner budget decision

Do not assume one 20 m non-stick roll will cover the entire run if every liner is discarded.

- At 40 cm per mask, **220 masks require 88 m**.
- Full single-use non-stick coverage requires **five 20 m rolls at £7.98 = £39.90**.
- Replacing the £12.03 plain-foil supply with those five rolls would bring the main materials to **£152.65**, or **£157.65 with the £5 allowance**, before prototype losses.
- Therefore the current £150 plan depends on **successful plain-foil release, demonstrated reuse of non-stick liners, or a revised lower-cost release supply**. The £132.76 initial list includes both the plain-foil allowance and one non-stick test roll; they are not two layers that must be used together.

A 40 cm cut is only a planning allowance. Deep faces may require more length or width. Count prototype foil use against the spare/test stock and recalculate from actual cuts.
